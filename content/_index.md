## About Me

I'm a researcher working at the intersection of theory and high-performance computing, with a focus on sparse linear algebra. I'm interested in working with mathematical abstractions that give us new insights into sparse structures, and in building tools for high-performance computing powered by those mathematical insights.

Currently I'm an MS student at Princeton, where I work with Mae Milano and Grace Dinh on communication lower bounds for sparse linear algebra.

## Publications

### Information-Theoretic Communication Lower Bounds for Sparse Matrix Multiplication 
Grace Dinh, Vivienne Goyal, Raye Kimmerer, James Demmel, Zhiru Zhang

*Submitted to SPAA '27*

Developed a novel method for programmatically finding communication lower bounds for nested loops accessing arrays, and derived new communication lower bounds for SpGEMM in both sequential and parallel machine models.

## Experience

### Research

#### Graduate Student Researcher -- Sparse Linear Algebra 
*Princeton University*

Developed structure-sensitive communication lower bounds for sparse matrix multiplication, based on information-theoretic techniques adapted from worst-case-optimal join methods in database theory.
Currently working on extending those techniques to format-aware cost modelling.

#### Research Apprentice -- Data Access and Visualisation 
*Lawrence Berkeley National Lab*

Built an API and web app to programatically expose data and visualisations from DESI (Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument), capable of highly-optimised querying and filtering. Serves data from the Perlmutter supercomputer at NERSC.
